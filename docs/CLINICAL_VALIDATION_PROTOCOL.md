# Clinical Validation Protocol

## Objective

Evaluate whether the app can safely support amblyopia/lazy eye screening workflows by comparing app screening outputs with standard pediatric eye-care examinations. Validation is required before clinical claims or public medical release.

## Study Design

- Prospective observational comparison study.
- App screening performed by parent/guardian or trained assistant.
- Clinical examination performed by pediatric ophthalmologist, ophthalmologist, or qualified optometrist.
- App result and clinical result compared using pre-defined metrics.
- App output remains screening/support only during the study.

## Inclusion Criteria

- Child or teen with guardian consent.
- Age group covered by the app workflow.
- Able to attempt age-appropriate screening steps with guardian support.
- Device type and environment documented.

## Exclusion Criteria

- Medical emergency requiring immediate care before app testing.
- Child unable to participate safely.
- Guardian declines consent.
- Device or environment cannot meet minimum testing conditions.
- Prior condition where clinician decides app testing is inappropriate.

## Age Groups

- 0-1 years
- 1-3 years
- 3-5 years
- 6-12 years
- Teens

## Device Groups

- Android phone
- iPhone
- Tablet
- Laptop/desktop screen

## Environment Variables To Record

- Lighting condition: bright, moderate, dim, glare/reflection present.
- Screen brightness level if available.
- Device model and browser.
- Calibration method and timestamp.
- Viewing distance.
- With glasses / without glasses.
- Parent/assistant involvement level.

## Tests To Compare

- Visual acuity.
- Red reflex.
- Alignment/Hirschberg proxy.
- Gaze/fixation proxy.
- Stereo/depth proxy if available.
- Patching adherence workflow only when a clinician-created plan exists.

## Clinical Gold Standard

- Pediatric ophthalmologist or qualified optometrist examination.
- Monocular visual acuity using age-appropriate clinical method.
- Cycloplegic refraction if available or clinically indicated.
- Cover/uncover and alternate cover testing.
- Ocular motility assessment.
- Red reflex, slit-lamp, and fundus examination when needed.
- Clinician assessment of media opacity, leukocoria concern, strabismus, refractive risk, and amblyopia risk.

## Metrics

- Sensitivity for clinically relevant screening concern.
- Specificity.
- False positive rate.
- False negative rate.
- Incomplete rate.
- Unreliable result rate.
- Urgent review trigger rate.
- Usability completion rate.
- Parent comprehension and task-completion feedback.
- Doctor review agreement with app triage state.

## Safety Handling

- Urgent referral: any white-pupil-like, absent red reflex, abnormal red reflex, severe vision concern, trauma, pain, or parent concern must receive clinical escalation.
- Incomplete result: treated as not reassuring and routed to review/retry.
- Parent concern: should override app reassurance and recommend eye-care review.
- AI uncertainty: route to review when quality or model uncertainty is high.
- Adverse events or unsafe wording must pause pilot enrollment until reviewed.

## Consent Requirements

- Guardian consent for child participation.
- Clear explanation that the app is not a diagnosis.
- Permission for app data to be compared with clinical findings.
- Permission for doctor review and report generation.
- Separate consent for any image/video capture if used.

## Data Privacy Requirements

- Collect only required screening, device, calibration, and review data.
- Protect child health data with role-based access.
- De-identify data for analysis when possible.
- Audit report exports and doctor actions.
- Define retention, deletion, and data access procedures.

## Limitations

- User-assisted calibration may vary across homes/devices.
- Parent-assisted occlusion can be wrong or inconsistent.
- Device cameras and screens vary.
- App screening is not equivalent to cycloplegic refraction or full eye examination.
- Study findings may not generalize to all settings without broader validation.

## Final Statement

Clinical validation is required before public medical claims. Until validation is completed and reviewed, the app remains a screening/support tool only.
